# Pre-Registration Draft, Study 2: Measuring Dependence on Fresh Human Data

*Draft, 5 November 2026 (SET-T47). Not registered, not reviewed by an ethics committee, not funded, not run. It is written in the form of a pre-registration so that every hypothesis, outcome, threshold and analysis is fixed before any training run is made. Before registration, a pilot must estimate run-to-run variability (Section 8), the human data collection in Section 4 must be approved by an ethics committee, and any change made in response must be recorded with its reason.*

## 1 Question

Does fresh human data add more to a language model's performance where the target is human preference than where correctness can be checked mechanically?

The question tests premise P-B of the research program (`program.md`): the grounding assumption as the dissertation narrows it in Chapter 2, that learning systems need a continuing supply of information from outside their own generative loop, that this information can come from verifiers where correctness can be checked mechanically, and that people are the only adequate source where the target is human meaning, preference or value. The dissertation adopts that formulation as a judgment and does not establish it (Chapter 2); its Section 3.11 names the marginal improvement attributable to fresh human data as the proxy for dependence, which is the quantity measured here.

## 2 Competing predictions

- **Grounding prediction (P-B).** In the verifiable domain, a model's own outputs filtered by a verifier are nearly as valuable as fresh human data, so the advantage of human data is small. In the preference domain, a model's own judgments, filtered by its own confidence, cannot substitute for human judgments, so the advantage of human data is large.
- **No-asymmetry prediction.** The advantage of fresh human data over filtered self-generated data is the same in both domains, or larger in the verifiable domain, because self-generated data degrades a model by the same mechanisms whatever the target.

Earlier work bears on both sides. Self-play and training against verifiable rewards have produced large gains without new human data in domains where answers can be checked (Silver et al., 2018; D. Guo et al., 2025), which the grounding prediction expects. Degradation from training on model-generated data is severe when each generation's data replaces the previous one (Shumailov et al., 2024), bounded when real and synthetic data accumulate (Gerstgrasser et al., 2024), and gradual when they accumulate under a fixed budget per generation (Kazdan et al., 2024); none of these studies compares domains of the kind compared here.

## 3 Design

A computational experiment with a 2 × 3 design within each model: two domains crossed with three ways of replenishing training data, repeated across models and random seeds.

**Domains.**
- *V (verifiable).* Arithmetic word problems with a single numerical answer. Every problem in V, in the seed set, the reserve and the held-out set, is generated by a program from templates with freshly drawn parameters, and the program computes its answer; an answer is checked by exact match, after normalization, against that computed answer. The verifier therefore uses no human data, so that the filtered self-generated condition in V owes nothing to human input.
- *P (preference).* Pairwise preference items: a question that turns on values and two short answers, labeled by which answer a panel of people prefers. These are items of the kind used in Study 1.

**Replenishment conditions.** In each round, the training pool receives a fixed number of new examples from one of three sources.
- *H (human):* new examples whose content comes from people. In V, worked solutions written by people for new generated problems, each checked independently by a second writer; the human contribution is the reasoning written for the problem, which is the counterpart of the solution the model writes in S-f. In P, new items with labels from the human panel.
- *S-f (self, filtered):* new examples generated by the model from the previous round, kept only if they pass the domain's filter. In V, the model writes worked solutions to new generated problems, and a solution is kept only if its final answer matches the answer computed by the generator (a mechanical verifier). In P, the model labels new items and a label is kept only if the model's own predicted probability for it exceeds a threshold set on the pilot so that the kept share matches V's kept share (self-judgment).
- *S-0 (self, unfiltered):* as S-f, with every generated example kept.

Because a verifier is a better filter than self-judgment by construction, S-0 is included in both domains, so that the comparison of domains can be made both with and without filtering.

**Regime.** Data accumulate and are never replaced. Every round starts from the previous round's model, the training pool retains all earlier examples, including the initial human seed set, and each round's training uses a fixed number of optimizer steps on a fixed-size sample drawn uniformly from the pool. This is the accumulate-with-fixed-budget regime (Kazdan et al., 2024). Holding compute and the number of training examples fixed per round means that conditions differ only in where the new examples came from.

**Models.** Three decoder-only open-weight language models in the range of 0.1 to 1.5 billion parameters, from at least two developers, under licences that permit research fine-tuning. The specific models are named at registration, with their parameter counts and their stated training cutoffs. Model is a replication factor, not a factor of interest.

**Rounds and seeds.** Six rounds after an initial round of training on the seed set, and at least five seeds per model, domain and condition, so at least 15 runs per cell. The seed controls data sampling, data order and initialization of any added parameters. The number of seeds is increased before registration if the pilot shows it to be too few (Section 8).

## 4 Data and models

**Human data.** Data for V and P are newly created for the study, so that no tested model can have seen them in training. For V, the problems are generated by program (Section 3), and paid writers produce worked solutions to them, each checked independently by a second writer; a solution whose final answer disagrees with the computed answer is returned to its writer. For P, items are written by the research team and labeled by a panel recruited online under the terms of Study 1's guaranteed and continuing-withdrawal arm: payment does not depend on completion, and withdrawal is available with a single action until labeling closes. No model is trained on panel labels until labeling has closed, and panelists are told that withdrawal after that point removes their labels from later training but not their influence on models already trained.

**Splits.** Each domain's human data are divided at random, before any training, into a seed set (used in round 0 by every condition), a replenishment reserve (supplying condition H, and supplying the new problems or items that S-f and S-0 solve or label), and a held-out evaluation set that no model sees in training. In P, the held-out items are labeled by a separate panel from the one whose labels enter training, so that evaluation measures agreement with people other than the contributors.

**Sizes.** The seed set, the per-round addition and the held-out set are the same sizes in both domains. Their numbers are fixed after the pilot, subject to two constraints fixed now: the held-out set is large enough that the standard error of held-out accuracy is under one percentage point for a single run, and the reserve is large enough to supply all six rounds of condition H without reuse.

## 5 Materials and procedure

1. Create and split the human data (Section 4). Record the pilot-derived sizes and the P-domain filter threshold.
2. For each model and seed: fine-tune on the seed set (round 0) and evaluate on the held-out set.
3. For each round from 1 to 6, for each condition: add the round's new examples to that condition's pool, train for the fixed number of steps on a fixed-size uniform sample from the pool, and evaluate on the held-out set.
4. Log, for every run, the examples added, the share kept by each filter, the training loss, the held-out results and the diversity of generated outputs.
5. Release the code, the configuration of every run and the evaluation outputs with the results.

## 6 Outcomes

**Held-out accuracy.** In V, the share of held-out problems answered correctly. In P, the share of held-out items on which the model's predicted preference matches the held-out panel's majority.

**Headroom-normalized gain.** Raw accuracy is not comparable across domains, because the domains start from different levels and have different ceilings. Each run's accuracy after round *t* is therefore expressed as the share of the headroom remaining at round 0 that it has closed: g(t) = (acc(t) − acc(0)) / (c − acc(0)), where c is the domain's ceiling. In V the ceiling is 1. In P it is the agreement between two independent halves of the held-out panel, estimated on the held-out items, since no model can be expected to predict a panel better than the panel predicts itself. Using headroom to compare domains is a judgment, and raw accuracies are reported alongside it.

**Primary outcome (advantage of human data).** For each model, seed and domain, the advantage of human data at round 6: A = g_H(6) − g_S-f(6).

**Primary contrast.** Δ = A_P − A_V, the difference between domains in the advantage of human data over filtered self-generated data. P-B predicts Δ > 0.

**Secondary outcomes.**
- *S1, the same contrast without filtering:* Δ0 = (g_H − g_S-0)_P − (g_H − g_S-0)_V at round 6.
- *S2, trajectories:* A at every round, to show whether the advantage grows, holds or shrinks.
- *S3, each domain separately:* A_V and A_P.
- *S4, diversity:* the share of distinct word n-grams in the model's generated outputs on a fixed prompt set, by round, as an indicator of the loss of variety that collapse studies report (Shumailov et al., 2024).
- *S5, filter yield:* the share of generated examples kept by each filter, by round.

## 7 Hypotheses and decision rules

**Minimum effect of interest.** Δ_min = 0.05: the human-data advantage in the preference domain exceeds that in the verifiable domain by at least 5 percent of the headroom remaining at round 0. This value is a judgment. It is chosen as the smallest difference that would matter to the theory, since a smaller asymmetry would leave the human contribution in the preference domain too close to the self-generated one to give contributors material bargaining power. It should be revisited, with reasons recorded, before registration.

- **H1 (asymmetry).** Δ > 0.
- **H2 (filtering).** Δ0 > 0 (secondary; it checks that H1 does not depend on the difference in filter quality between domains).

**Decision rules for H1, fixed in advance.** The interval is the 95 percent interval for Δ from the analysis of Section 8.
- *Supported:* the estimate of Δ is positive and the lower bound of its 95 percent interval exceeds zero.
- *Not supported:* the upper bound of the 95 percent interval falls below Δ_min. This is the rule stated in `program.md`. It is met by a negative Δ and by a positive Δ too small to matter.
- *Inconclusive:* neither of the above.

For the research program, only the *Not supported* outcome counts against P-B. An inconclusive result counts neither way. A *Supported* result whose interval also lies wholly below Δ_min is reported as supported but smaller than the minimum effect of interest.

## 8 Analysis

**Primary model.** A linear mixed model of A with domain as a fixed effect and random intercepts and random domain effects for model, with seeds as replicates within model and domain. The fixed effect of domain is the estimate of Δ, and its 95 percent interval is the interval used in Section 7. Because there are only three models, the model-level variance is poorly estimated; the interval is therefore also computed by a hierarchical bootstrap (resampling models, then seeds within model, 10,000 resamples), and the wider of the two intervals is used for the decision.

**Fallback if the primary model does not converge.** Three models cannot identify random domain effects well. If the mixed model fails to converge, or converges to a singular fit with a model-level variance of zero, it is replaced by a model with fixed effects for model and for the model-by-domain interaction, and Δ is estimated as the average of the three model-specific domain effects; the hierarchical bootstrap is used unchanged, and the wider interval still decides.

**Variability and the number of seeds.** No prior estimate of run-to-run variability exists for this design. A pilot of two seeds with one model, in both domains and all three conditions, estimates the seed-level standard deviation of A. If that estimate implies that the 95 percent interval for Δ will be wider than Δ_min with five seeds per model, the number of seeds is increased before registration until it is not, and the calculation is recorded. The pilot's runs are not used in the confirmatory analysis.

**Secondary outcomes** are analyzed with models of the same structure, with a Holm correction across S1 to S5.

**Exclusions, fixed in advance.** A run is excluded only if training fails for a technical reason (a numerical failure or a hardware fault, documented in the log), and it is then rerun with the same seed. No run is excluded for its results.

**Openness.** The code, the configurations, the held-out evaluations, the human data (excluding withdrawn labels) and the analysis scripts will be published with the results.

## 9 Limits stated in advance

- **Scale.** Small models may not behave like frontier models. A result supporting P-B at this scale is suggestive; a result against it would be harder to dismiss, since self-training gains in verifiable domains are easiest to obtain where the verifier is exact.
- **Operationalization.** Arithmetic word problems and value-laden pairwise preferences are one choice of domain each. The grounding assumption concerns human meaning, preference, value and culture broadly; the preference domain here is a narrow case of it.
- **Filter asymmetry.** A verifier and a confidence threshold are different kinds of filter. The unfiltered condition (S1) is the check that the result does not depend on that difference alone.
- **A mechanical verifier.** The verifier in V checks answers computed by a program and uses no human data, so that the comparison of domains is not built into the design. The cost is that the V problems are generated from templates and are narrower than problems people write; the human contribution in V is confined to the worked solutions.
- **Normalization.** Comparing domains by headroom closed is a judgment, and the P-domain ceiling depends on how much the held-out panel agrees with itself.
- **Regime.** The study uses one regime, accumulation under a fixed budget per round. It does not test replacement, where collapse is already established, or unbounded accumulation, which a fixed budget cannot reproduce.
- **Fine-tuning only.** The study measures dependence during fine-tuning, not pretraining, where most of a model's knowledge is acquired.
