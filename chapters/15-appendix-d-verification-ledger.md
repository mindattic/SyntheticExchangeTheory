# Appendix D: Source Verification Ledger

This dissertation follows a strict rule: no source is cited as if it had been checked unless it was. Every work in the reference list was located in this project's research passes (October 2026) through its publisher page, DOI registry record, a library or repository record, or its full text. This appendix records the level at which each was verified, so that a reader or examiner can see exactly how much weight each citation bears. The full research notes, with the specific passages checked and the claims each source was used to support, are preserved in the project's `references/` directory.

**Levels.**

- **Full text read.** The work itself (or, where noted, the relevant chapters or sections) was opened and the cited content was checked against it.
- **Abstract or publisher page read.** The abstract, publisher landing page, official summary or equivalent was read; the body was not. Claims attributed to these works are confined to what such pages establish.
- **Record located.** The bibliographic record was confirmed but the content was not read. No work in the final reference list is at this level (see D.3).

All DOIs in the reference list were taken from Crossref records or publisher pages; a sample was re-resolved against the Crossref API during compilation.

## D.4 Corrections made to the working draft

The verification passes found and corrected the following errors in the working draft from which this dissertation was developed.

1. The study cited as finding that a language model "introduced nearly nine times more new vulnerabilities than they fixed" compares vulnerabilities introduced by the model with those introduced by human developers; it does not count vulnerabilities fixed, and it studies ordinary issue resolution rather than security patching (Sajadi et al., 2025).
2. The "FLAWED" pattern was attributed to a secondary blog; its source is an industry white paper (Mierczuk et al., 2026), now cited directly and identified as non-peer-reviewed.
3. An AI-generated, unreviewed industry report was cited for the concept of software monoculture; the concept is now sourced to Geer et al. (2003) and Stamp (2004).
4. A sovereign-AI figure ("21 projects as of June 2026") could not be found in its cited source, which tracks 184 projects in 67 countries (Chavez et al., 2026).
5. The Wörgl currency experiment was described as having "measurably reduced local unemployment"; the evidence does not support that claim (September, 2026; Greco, 2002; Fisher, 1933).
6. The information-gain data-valuation paper was misattributed to "Hoang et al."; its authors are Sim, Zhang, Chan and Low (2020).
7. Shannon (1948) was cited as defining a unit of information *processed*; Shannon defines a unit of information, and the precedent for a processing unit is benchmark methodology (Mattson, Cheng, et al., 2020; Reddi et al., 2020).
8. Consumer-hardware claims cited to enthusiast websites and a blog are now sourced to Micron Technology (2025) and TrendForce (2025, 2026a, 2026b); an unverified claim about a shelved consumer GPU was removed.
9. The model-collapse result in *Physical Review Letters* was overstated: real data must be combined with regularization or a prior, and the "single data point" result holds only in specific examples (Jangjoo et al., 2026).
10. The claim that robotic data is "not human-generated" was corrected: large real-world robot datasets are still gathered largely through human demonstration (Khazatsky et al., 2024). (Item 33 records a further correction to this sentence.)
11. An informal blog post on a "95% robotics data gap" was removed; the number has no peer-reviewed source.
12. The open-weight model was claimed to make "every input auditable"; leading open-weight models do not disclose their training data (Touvron et al., 2023; Bommasani et al., 2023).
13. An organoid review was cited for claims its abstract does not contain; those claims are now sourced to primary research (Smirnova et al., 2023; Kagan et al., 2022; Cai et al., 2023; Robbins et al., 2026) and the National Academies (2021).
14. "Ibarra et al." corrected to Arrieta-Ibarra et al. (2018); Ostrom (1990), cited but missing from the reference list, added.
15. Rawls's original position was described as a negotiation in which designers "might land on either side"; that reasoning is Harsanyi's, and the mechanism actually proposed is closer to Buchanan and Tullock's veil of uncertainty. Chapter 5 now says so.
16. Only four of Ostrom's eight design principles were used, and one was mis-mapped; all eight are now mapped (Chapter 5).

A second, independent review of the completed manuscript compared every factual claim in the empirical chapters against the research notes and found a further set of problems, all now corrected. The most significant were these.

17. A finding about trust and fairness in reciprocal exchange was attributed to Molm (2003), whose cited article establishes only that the form of exchange changes how power is used; the claim was reduced to what the article supports.

19. Appendix A claimed that an unconditional survival floor raises the human party's bargained payoff by an additional half of its value in every exchange. A floor received whether or not agreement is reached raises both disagreement and agreement payoffs equally and leaves the division of surplus unchanged; the half-value shift arises only where the floor substitutes for something the counterparty would otherwise have supplied. Appendix A, Chapter 3 and Chapter 10 now say so.
20. The sovereign-AI index was cited for a claim about the cultural motives of sovereign projects that it does not make, and for a claim about "most national efforts" that holds only for model projects disclosing their base; both are corrected.
21. The Wörgl experiment's end was attributed to the Administrative Court on the strength of sources that do not name it; the court is now sourced to the Vienna University of Economics and Business exhibition page, with the conflicting dates noted.
22. The MLPerf time-to-train claim was cited to the overview article rather than to the training-benchmark paper, and stated as a "finding" rather than an argument; both are corrected.
