Dear Editors,

I am pleased to submit the original research article "Terms, Not Character: The Priority of Exit in Exchange Between People and Artificial Systems" for consideration in *Philosophy & Technology*.

Discussion of advanced artificial intelligence asks, above all, whether the systems will be good. The article argues that this question is insufficient, and develops a complementary account centred on the terms of exchange between people and the systems they depend on. Drawing on power-dependence theory, it defines three conditions of fair exchange (consent, reciprocal value and real exit) and shows that they are ordered by priority. Voluntary consent presupposes the ability to refuse without falling below a survival floor, and exit is what disciplines reciprocity. Exit alone can neither inform a party nor make terms fair. A bargaining analysis explains why such a floor should be an inalienable disagreement payoff rather than an outside option, and corrects a common overstatement of what a floor does to the division of surplus. The article also shows that the dependence of artificial systems on human input makes fair terms only conditionally self-enforcing, and that, when this dependence is shared among many systems, it becomes a commons.

I believe the article suits the journal's interest in the philosophical questions raised where technology meets social and political life. It joins philosophy of technology with exchange theory, bargaining theory and the philosophy of consent and exploitation, and it states its claims in a form precise enough to be contested.

The manuscript is original, has not been published elsewhere, and is not under consideration by another journal. The article was developed with the assistance of a large language model, as described in the manuscript's note on method and in the declarations on the title page; no language model is listed as an author. The manuscript has been anonymized for double-anonymized review, and identifying information appears only on the separate title page.

Thank you for considering this submission.
