# Sources verified directly by the lead author session

### CalabresiMelamed1972
- Calabresi, G., & Melamed, A. D. (1972). Property rules, liability rules, and inalienability: One view of the cathedral. *Harvard Law Review*, 85(6), 1089–1128. https://doi.org/10.2307/1340059
- Verification: record located (Stanford law library, JSTOR 1340059, Columbia-hosted PDF)
- Content: entitlements protected by property rules (consent of holder required), liability rules (taking permitted with externally set compensation), or inalienability rules (transfer barred even with consent).
- Use: survival tier as an inalienability rule; discretionary tier as property-rule protected.

### Radin1987
- Radin, M. J. (1987). Market-inalienability. *Harvard Law Review*, 100(8), 1849–1937. https://doi.org/10.2307/1341192
- Verification: record located (author CV; reprint excerpt)
- Content: some things may be given but not sold; commodification of personhood-constitutive goods threatens flourishing; critique of "universal commodification".
- Use: why the survival tier can be non-tradable without being paternalistic tyranny; why data pricing must avoid commodifying persons.

### Nash1950
- Nash, J. F., Jr. (1950). The bargaining problem. *Econometrica*, 18(2), 155–162. https://doi.org/10.2307/1907266
- Verification: record located (Econometric Society; JSTOR 1907266)
- Use: bargaining outcome depends on the disagreement point.

### BinmoreRubinsteinWolinsky1986
- Binmore, K., Rubinstein, A., & Wolinsky, A. (1986). The Nash bargaining solution in economic modelling. *RAND Journal of Economics*, 17(2), 176–188. https://doi.org/10.2307/2555382
- Verification: abstract read (RePEc)
- Content: strategic alternating-offers bargaining converges to the Nash solution as frictions vanish, with the disagreement point chosen appropriately (impasse payoffs vs. breakdown).

### BinmoreShakedSutton1989
- Binmore, K., Shaked, A., & Sutton, J. (1989). An outside option experiment. *Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 104(4), 753–770. https://doi.org/10.2307/2937866
- Verification: abstract read (LSE Research Online)
- Content: outside options affect the bargain only if credible ("outside option principle"); treating them as disagreement payoffs is naive; experiment supports the strategic prediction.
- Use: distinction between an income floor received *during* impasse (disagreement payoff, always shifts the bargain) and an exit option (binds only when exercise is credible).

### Hume1742
- Hume, D. (1987). Of the independency of parliament. In E. F. Miller (Ed.), *Essays, moral, political, and literary* (pp. 42–46). Liberty Fund. (Original work published 1742)
- Verification: text located (Econlib Liberty Fund edition; Gutenberg)
- Content: political maxim that in designing government every man ought to be supposed a knave; Hume thinks the supposition false in fact but correct for design.

### Federalist51
- Madison, J. (1788). The Federalist No. 51. *New York Packet*, February 8, 1788. Avalon Project, Yale Law School. https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/fed51.asp
- Verification: full text read (Avalon). Signed Publius; Avalon heading reads "Hamilton or Madison"; modern scholarship attributes to Madison.
- Quotes: "If men were angels, no government would be necessary." "Ambition must be made to counteract ambition."

### WilsonOstromCox2013
- Wilson, D. S., Ostrom, E., & Cox, M. E. (2013). Generalizing the core design principles for the efficacy of groups. *Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization*, 90(Suppl.), S21–S32. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jebo.2012.12.010
- Verification: abstract read (author site, MPG OpenEvo listing)
- Content: Ostrom's CPR design principles are derivable from evolutionary dynamics of cooperation and apply to groups beyond natural-resource management (schools, neighborhood groups); group performance tracked compliance with principles.
- Use: licenses applying Ostrom to a non-natural commons such as compute/data.

### FunkeSchularickTrebesch2016
- Funke, M., Schularick, M., & Trebesch, C. (2016). Going to extremes: Politics after financial crises, 1870–2014. *European Economic Review*, 88, 227–260. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.euroecorev.2016.03.006
- Verification: abstract read (RePEc); DOI confirmed via Crossref.
- Content: 20 advanced economies, 800+ elections, ~140 years. After financial crises, policy uncertainty rises, government majorities shrink, polarization increases; far-right vote share rises ~30% on average. No comparable effect after normal recessions or non-financial macro shocks.

### deBromhead2013
- de Bromhead, A., Eichengreen, B., & O'Rourke, K. H. (2013). Political extremism in the 1920s and 1930s: Do German lessons generalize? *Journal of Economic History*, 73(2), 371–406. https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022050713000302
- Verification: abstract read (search summary of publisher/IDEAS); DOI confirmed via Crossref.
- Content: economic hard times (cumulative growth more than election-year growth) linked to far-right vote; effect strongest where democracy was young, representation thresholds low, and countries lost WWI.

### PerzanowskiSchultz2016
- Perzanowski, A., & Schultz, J. (2016). *The end of ownership: Personal property in the digital economy*. MIT Press.
- Verification: publisher/author pages and reviews read (search). Content: retailers and rightsholders characterize digital purchases as licenses; Amazon's remote deletion of Kindle copies of *1984* as example; licensing creates uncertainty about what buyers get.
