## A. Demurrage, stamp scrip, and Wörgl

### Gesell1916
- APA 7: Gesell, S. (1958). *The natural economic order* (P. Pye, Trans.; rev. ed.). Peter Owen. (Original work published 1916 as *Die natürliche Wirtschaftsordnung durch Freiland und Freigeld*, Bern and Berlin; first English translation 1929, Berlin: NEO-Verlag.)
- Link: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Natural_Economic_Order (bibliographic history); library record of the Pye translation: https://dspace.gipe.ac.in/xmlui/handle/10973/22327 ; full text (1958 text): https://theanarchistlibrary.org/library/silvio-gesell-the-natural-economic-order
- Verification: record located only. Edition history was confirmed from multiple catalogue and bibliographic records. The text itself was not read against a specific claim in this session.
- What it actually establishes: Gesell's book combined "Free Land" (land nationalisation) with "Free Money" (*Freigeld*): currency that loses value unless a periodic fee is paid, so that money carries a holding cost like other goods. It grew out of two earlier works: *Die Verwirklichung des Rechtes auf den vollen Arbeitsertrag* (1906) and *Die neue Lehre vom Zins* (1911). Pye's English translation was made from the 6th German edition, issued in 1929 by NEO-Verlag (a press set up for the purpose), and revised for the 1958 Peter Owen edition. A Texas edition by Hugo Fack (1934) is abridged.
- Use in dissertation: The primary source for demurrage. Cite the edition actually read (the 1958 Pye revision is the most common) with "Original work published 1916". Note that Gesell paired demurrage with land reform. Keynes's objection (below) turns on exactly that point: other liquid stores of value substitute for money.

### Keynes1936
- APA 7: Keynes, J. M. (1936). *The general theory of employment, interest and money*. Macmillan. (Chapter 23, "Notes on mercantilism, the usury laws, stamped money and theories of under-consumption," Section VI.)
- Link: https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/economics/keynes/general-theory/ch23.htm ; https://userpage.fu-berlin.de/~roehrigw/keynes/engl.htm
- Verification: full text read (Ch. 23 §VI, two online transcriptions).
- What it actually establishes: Keynes calls Gesell an "unduly neglected prophet". He writes that the future "will learn more from the spirit of Gesell than from that of Marx" and that "the idea behind stamped money is sound." He also identifies the key flaw: money is not the only asset with a liquidity premium. If currency bore a carrying cost, substitutes such as bank money, debts at call, foreign money, jewellery, precious metals, and land would take its place. Gesell's land nationalisation would remove land, but not the rest. Keynes adds that the right stamp rate would be roughly the excess of the money rate of interest over the marginal efficiency of capital at full employment. The two transcriptions disagree on Gesell's stamp rate. One reads "1 per mil. per week, equivalent to 5.2 per cent per annum"; the other reads "per month ... 5.4 per cent". Check the printed 1936 page before quoting a figure.
- Use in dissertation: The single most important scholarly endorsement of demurrage, and also its central critique. Keynes's substitution argument applies directly to the two-tier design: if discretionary compute is taxed while held, holders will convert surpluses into compute-generating hardware, equity in compute firms, or other assets. The draft already concedes this ("acquire more compute-generating capacity"). Keynes is the canonical citation for it.

### Fisher1933StampScrip
- APA 7: Fisher, I. (1933). *Stamp scrip*. Adelphi Company. (Usually credited as written with the assistance of H. R. L. Cohrssen and H. W. Fisher; the co-credit was not confirmed on a title page in this session.)
- Link: https://userpage.fu-berlin.de/~roehrigw/fisher/ (online transcription, chapters)
- Verification: full text read (Ch. IV on Wörgl, online transcription). Publisher and year were confirmed via bibliographies, including the Annals of Iowa and the depressionscrip.com bibliography.
- What it actually establishes: Fisher endorsed a temporary, local, dated stamp scrip as a way to raise velocity and "prime the pump", to be retired once recovery came. His Wörgl chapter reports that about 1,500 people in the town of about 4,300 and its surroundings were unemployed after factory closures in 1931–32. The 32,000-schilling issue (1-, 5-, and 10-schilling notes, August 1932) proved larger than needed, and a third or less circulated. Fisher reports rapid recirculation through tax payments (4,542 schillings of arrears collected in the first month) and about 100,000 schillings of public works in late 1932. He also records that **permanent jobs went to only about 30 to 50 of the unemployed**. Fisher was an advocate, not a neutral evaluator.
- Use in dissertation: The primary US-scholarly contemporary source on Wörgl. His own figures support "public works and tax recovery", not a large measured fall in unemployment.

### Champ2008
- APA 7: Champ, B. (2008). Stamp scrip: Money people paid to use. *Economic Commentary* (Federal Reserve Bank of Cleveland), April 1, 2008. https://ideas.repec.org/a/fip/fedcec/y2008iapr.html
- Link: https://www.clevelandfed.org/publications/economic-commentary/2008/ec-20080401-stamp-scrip-money-people-paid-to-use
- Verification: full text read. This is a Federal Reserve policy commentary, not peer-reviewed.
- What it actually establishes: Wörgl issued stamp scrip in August 1932 to finance public works. Accounts indicate "some success", and an Austrian court forbade issuance in November 1933. Champ gives no Wörgl unemployment figure. For the US, Harper counted 95 stamp-scrip issues (37 cities, 8 counties, plus business groups). Most were small ($300–$500), no issue above $2,000 succeeded, and the largest municipal issue (Polk County, Iowa) failed. Failures came from business reluctance, coordination problems, and undated scrip being experienced as a consumption tax. The Bankhead–Pettengill national stamp-scrip bill (Feb. 1933) never came to a vote. Champ's lesson is that people value a low-transaction-cost medium of exchange, and stamp scrip's cumbersomeness doomed it once ordinary money was available again. He links stamp scrip to Goodfriend's carry tax as a zero-lower-bound tool.
- Use in dissertation: An authoritative, balanced summary of stamp scrip. It shows that the US scaling attempts failed, and why. It also supports the design choice that demurrage on digital compute balances avoids the stamp-handling transaction costs that killed paper scrip.

### vonMuralt1934
- APA 7: von Muralt, A. (1934). The Woergl experiment with depreciating money. *Annals of Public and Cooperative Economics, 10*(1), 48–57. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8292.1934.tb00435.x
- Link: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8292.1934.tb00435.x
- Verification: record located only (Crossref metadata). Its content is known here through detailed secondary summaries in Greco (2002) and September (2026), both read in full. The article itself was not read.
- What it actually establishes (via those summaries): This is the most detailed contemporary investigation. It reports about 350 unemployed in Wörgl (200 without benefits) and about 1,500 in the surrounding district. Only about 12,000 schillings were actually issued and backed. Local tax revenue rose substantially, mainly from collecting arrears, but von Muralt says the increases were "not as substantial as those cited by the burgomaster" and need an impartial audit. Employment works were also financed by subsidies from the Productive Unemployment Fund and a 12,000-schilling relief credit from the Tyrol government. The parish's 1.29 million-schilling debt to the Innsbruck Savings Bank was unchanged. He reports no price rise.
- Use in dissertation: The best contemporary evidence base. Read it directly before citing numbers. Its point that outside money also funded the works is essential to any causal claim.

### Unterguggenberger1934
- APA 7: Unterguggenberger, M. (1934). The end results of the Woergl experiment. *Annals of Public and Cooperative Economics, 10*(1), 60–63. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8292.1934.tb00437.x
- Link: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8292.1934.tb00437.x ; reproduction: https://beyondmoney.net/the-end-results-of-the-worgl-experiment/
- Verification: record located only (Crossref). This is the mayor's own account, an interested party.
- What it actually establishes (as quoted in September 2026): The depreciating money was withdrawn from circulation on September 1, 1933, "under repeated orders of the supervisory authorities." The mayor lists the projects completed: roads, a concrete bridge, street lighting, a reservoir, and a ski jump.
- Use in dissertation: Primary source for the date and manner of termination. Treat the mayor's success claims as advocacy.

### Greco2002
- APA 7: Greco, T. H., Jr. (2002, May 9). *Comment on the Wörgl experiment with community currency and demurrage* [Unpublished essay]. https://base.socioeco.org/docs/doc-278_en.pdf
- Link: https://base.socioeco.org/docs/doc-278_en.pdf
- Verification: full text read. This is a non-peer-reviewed practitioner essay, flagged as such.
- What it actually establishes: Greco reviews the 1934 *Annals* articles (von Muralt, Unterguggenberger). He accepts that the local currency improved parish finances and local conditions, but argues that the evidence that *demurrage* caused the improvement is "far less compelling". He attributes most of the effect to three things: the currency's "tax foundation" (residents owed large tax arrears payable in the scrip), the local-only circulation, and outside subsidies. In his view, results "would have been largely the same without" demurrage.
- Use in dissertation: The clearest statement of the identification problem. The chapter currently credits demurrage specifically for Wörgl's outcomes, and this is the source to cite for the counter-argument.

### September2026
- APA 7: September, J. (2026). A sustainable miracle? Examining the long-term viability of the Wörgl experiment. *International Journal of Community Currency Research, 30*(1), 114–126. https://doi.org/10.26034/zh.ijccr.2026.9551
- Link: https://www.ijccr.net/article/download/9551/9197/26316
- Verification: full text read.
- What it actually establishes: This is the most recent scholarly review, with a careful source trail. It repeats the commonly cited outcome figures (unemployment fell 25% while rising 19% Austria-wide; tax revenues +34.4%; investment +219.7%) but traces them to secondary sources: Schwarz (1951), a Freiwirtschaft-movement publication; Ottacher (2002), a diploma thesis; and Broer (2007). None of these is an audited time series. The paper finds an unexplained inconsistency in the redemption data: 34,500 schillings of certificates were redeemed in 9 months against a reported 12,000-schilling reserve. It also reports that the daily exchange records were destroyed in the 1960s and many municipal accounts in WWII, so the question "will remain largely unanswered." On termination, the Austrian National Bank invoked Article 122 of the Nationalbank law, the mayor appealed to the courts in Vienna and lost, and the money was withdrawn on September 1, 1933.
- Use in dissertation: The key citation for an honest description of Wörgl: widely reported local improvement, weak and partly irreconcilable data, and no causal identification.

### WUWien-Woergl
- APA 7: Institut für Wirtschafts- und Sozialgeschichte, Wirtschaftsuniversität Wien. (n.d.). *Konstruktiver Ungehorsam* [Virtual exhibition "Freiwirtschaftliche Markierungen"]. https://www.wu.ac.at/geschichte/institut/forschung/virtuelle-ausstellung-freiwirtschaftliche-markierungen/konstruktiver-ungehorsam
- Link: as above
- Verification: full text read (an academic institute's exhibition page, not peer-reviewed).
- What it actually establishes: Wörgl issued "Arbeitswertscheine" with 1% monthly decay from mid-1932, supported by the local Raiffeisenkasse. Kirchbichl joined in January 1933, and other towns prepared similar schemes. Officials objected from early 1933 under Article 122 of the Nationalbankgesetz. The Dollfuss cabinet discussed it in summer 1933, and Finance Minister Buresch warned that bad money would drive out good. The Verwaltungsgerichtshof (Administrative Court) ended the experiment in mid-September 1933. Austria-wide unemployment rose about 20% in 1932–33, while Wörgl's labour market "improved noticeably" (no figure given). The page cites Broer (2007) and Franz Klein in *Der österreichische Volkswirt* (1933).
- Use in dissertation: A German-language academic source for the legal and political end of the experiment. Pair it with Broer (2007) for a monograph citation. Broer (2007), *Schwundgeld: Bürgermeister Michael Unterguggenberger und das Wörgler Währungsexperiment 1932/33* (StudienVerlag, Innsbruck), is record located only via bibliographies.

### Blanc1998
- APA 7: Blanc, J. (1998). Free money for social progress: Theory and practice of Gesell's accelerated money. *American Journal of Economics and Sociology, 57*(4), 469–483. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1536-7150.1998.tb03376.x

### Gelleri2009
- APA 7: Gelleri, C. (2009). Chiemgauer Regiomoney: Theory and practice of a local currency. *International Journal of Community Currency Research, 13*, 61–75. https://doi.org/10.15133/j.ijccr.2009.006
- Link: https://ijccr.net/2012/05/29/chiemgauer-regiomoney-theory-and-practice-of-a-local-currency ; PDF mirror: https://enviro.fss.muni.cz/media/3100367/theory-and-practice-of-a-local-currency.pdf
- Verification: full text read. Flag: the author is the Chiemgauer's founder and manager, and the text is based on a 2009 practitioner presentation, so it is not independent evaluation.
- What it actually establishes: The Chiemgauer (Bavaria, since 2003) is pegged 1:1 to the euro. Its demurrage is 2% per quarter (8% per year), paid by stamps. Businesses converting back to euros pay a 5% fee. Turnover was about €3.6 million in 2008 (+30%), and about 300,000 Chiemgauer were in circulation in January 2009. Gelleri claims, without a stated method, that velocity reached about three times the euro's after five years. He repeats an unsourced claim that Wörgl's unemployment "declined by 25%."
- Use in dissertation: Evidence that a modern demurrage currency can operate at small scale for years. It is not evidence of macro effects, and it should not be cited for Wörgl numbers.

### Godschalk2012
- APA 7: Godschalk, H. (2012). Does demurrage matter for complementary currencies? *International Journal of Community Currency Research, 16*(D), 58–69. https://doi.org/10.15133/j.ijccr.2012.012
- Link: https://www.ijccr.net/article/view/9173
- Verification: abstract/publisher page read. The journal page spells the author "Godshalk"; the standard spelling is Godschalk.
- What it actually establishes: Godschalk notes that demurrage has never been implemented on state money as Gesell intended. Drawing on Fisher and Depression-era experience, he argues it could work as a temporary anti-depression tool by raising velocity, probably only if issued state-wide. For local complementary currencies, the available data suggest the demurrage rate is "not crucial" for usage, turnover, or velocity.
- Use in dissertation: The strongest peer-reviewed counterpoint on demurrage. In local currencies, demurrage is not shown to matter much. The design should argue its case on anti-accumulation grounds, not velocity grounds, and acknowledge this.

### Gatch2012
- APA 7: Gatch, L. (2012). Tax anticipation scrip as a form of local currency in the USA during the 1930s. *International Journal of Community Currency Research, 16*(D). https://doi.org/10.15133/j.ijccr.2012.009
- Link: https://www.ijccr.net/article/view/9169
- Verification: abstract/publisher page read.
- What it actually establishes: The most significant US Depression scrip was not stamp scrip but tax-anticipation scrip. It was longer-lived and issued in larger amounts, and it circulated because local governments accepted it for taxes. Gatch links the episode to neo-chartalist accounts of money.
- Use in dissertation: Supports Greco's point that tax-receivability ("tax foundation") is what made municipal scrip work. For compute, the analogue is mandatory acceptance: what obligations can be discharged in compute?

### BuiterPanigirtzoglou2003
- APA 7: Buiter, W. H., & Panigirtzoglou, N. (2003). Overcoming the zero bound on nominal interest rates with negative interest on currency: Gesell's solution. *The Economic Journal, 113*(490), 723–746. https://doi.org/10.1111/1468-0297.t01-1-00162
- Link: https://doi.org/10.1111/1468-0297.t01-1-00162 ; working paper: https://www.nber.org/papers/w7245
- Verification: abstract/publisher page read (RePEc/LSE abstracts and Crossref metadata).
- What it actually establishes: In Old- and New-Keynesian models where nominal rates can get stuck at zero, an augmented rule that keeps the rate on base money at or below other rates removes the zero-bound equilibria. Implementing it requires negative interest on currency, a Gesell carry tax. The authors weigh the administrative costs of taxing currency against benefits, including lower shoe-leather and menu costs and closing output gaps.
- Use in dissertation: Mainstream peer-reviewed legitimation of demurrage as monetary engineering. Note that its purpose is macro-stabilisation (escaping the zero bound), not wealth redistribution. The draft's redistributive rationale is a different claim that this paper does not test.

### Goodfriend2000
- APA 7: Goodfriend, M. (2000). Overcoming the zero bound on interest rate policy. *Journal of Money, Credit and Banking, 32*(4), 1007–1035. https://doi.org/10.2307/2601157
- Link: https://ideas.repec.org/a/mcb/jmoncb/v32y2000i4p1007-35.html
- Verification: abstract/publisher page read (RePEc abstract; DOI via Crossref).
- What it actually establishes: Goodfriend examines three options at the zero bound: a variable carry tax on electronic bank reserves, a carry tax on currency, and quantitative policy (monetary transfers, long-bond purchases). A central bank pursuing the last at the floor would need more Treasury support than usual.
- Use in dissertation: Shows that a carry tax is technically straightforward on electronic balances, which is the relevant case for ledger-based compute.

### Mankiw2009
- APA 7: Mankiw, N. G. (2009, April 19). It may be time for the Fed to go negative. *The New York Times*. https://www.nytimes.com/2009/04/19/business/economy/19view.html
- Link: as above; author's pointer: https://gregmankiw.blogspot.com/2009/04/ ("Going Negative", April 18, 2009)
- Verification: record located only. The NYT blocked fetch, so the column was located via the author's own blog post linking it, and the summary comes from his follow-up posts. Newspaper op-ed, flagged.
- What it actually establishes: Mankiw argued that a Taylor rule implied a federal funds rate near −1% in 2009. He floated, tongue-in-cheek, a lottery voiding currency by serial number, to show that zero is not a hard lower bound.
- Use in dissertation: Optional popular-press illustration that carry taxes on money are discussed by mainstream economists. Prefer Goodfriend (2000) and Buiter & Panigirtzoglou (2003) for scholarly weight.

## B. Sovereign AI, competition, and hardware supply

### CNAS2026
- APA 7: Chavez, P., Chilukuri, V., & Scanlon, R. (2026, August 20). *Sovereign AI's second wave is coming into view* (CNAS Insights). Center for a New American Security. https://www.cnas.org/publications/cnas-insights/cnas-insights-sovereign-ais-second-wave-is-coming-into-view
- Link: as above
- Verification: full text read. This is a think-tank publication, not peer-reviewed.
- What it actually establishes: The CNAS Sovereign AI Index (v2.0, data through H1 2026) tracks **184 government-backed projects across 67 countries** plus the EU, including 41 new projects in H1 2026. Forty percent of tracked countries back a single project, and ten countries account for nearly 40% of projects. India leads with 11. Infrastructure projects made up 80% of new projects in H1 2026. Among model projects with disclosed bases, "most" are fine-tuned foreign open-weight models, and three-fifths of those bases are American (Llama, then Mistral and Gemma). From-scratch models reached about 40% of model projects, a new high, but they "lag far behind the frontier", and about two-thirds have fewer than 70B parameters. Only a handful of jurisdictions (India, South Korea, the UK, Taiwan) have operational programmes across compute, models, and data together.
- Use in dissertation: Supports the qualitative claim that most sovereign efforts adapt foreign open models and that frontier capability is concentrated. **It does not support "21 such projects as of June 2026."** No figure of 21 appears in the piece. See Corrections.

### OECD2025
- APA 7: OECD. (2025). *Competition in artificial intelligence infrastructure* (OECD Roundtables on Competition Policy Papers, No. 330). OECD Publishing. https://doi.org/10.1787/623d1874-en
- Link: https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/competition-in-artificial-intelligence-infrastructure_623d1874-en.html (PDF: https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/11/competition-in-artificial-intelligence-infrastructure_69319aee/623d1874-en.pdf)
- Verification: full text read (searched and read the executive summary and §4.1 on collusion). Published November 2025 as background for the 147th Competition Committee meeting (O.N.E. ref. DAF/COMP(2025)11). This is a Secretariat paper and does not necessarily reflect member-country views.
- What it actually establishes: AI compute markets combine high concentration and entry barriers, vertical and conglomerate integration, cross-holdings and partnerships, heavy state intervention, and demand outstripping supply. Enforcement "has been limited so far". The main concerns are exclusionary conduct (bundling, foreclosure through exclusivity), acqui-hires, and partnerships below merger thresholds. On collusion, the paper says the risk is **relatively low at the leading edge**, because asymmetric shares and fast innovation make cartels unlikely, but **higher in matured, commoditised layers**. Box 5 recounts the 1998–2002 DRAM price-fixing cartel. It flags **bid-rigging risk in large data-centre, fibre, and energy construction tenders**, especially public-private ones with compressed timelines, and recommends better procurement design.
- Use in dissertation: The draft's "regulators already flag bid-rigging and collusion risk" is **accurate but needs qualifying**. The OECD flags it as a prospective risk, not observed conduct; the bid-rigging point concerns infrastructure *construction procurement*; and the report rates frontier collusion risk as low. It also matters for Problem 4: a New-Deal-style public compute build-out is exactly where the OECD says bid-rigging risk concentrates, so the chapter should build procurement safeguards into the proposal.

### Telecom1996-254

He states that "semantic aspects of communication are irrelevant to the engineering problem." Information is measured logarithmically over the set of possible messages. Shannon's explicit exclusion of meaning also sits awkwardly with pricing data by its "value" to a model.

### Lloyd2000

## Corrections to the draft

1. **Wörgl, "measurably reduced local unemployment."** This overstates the evidence. The commonly cited figures (a 25% local fall against a 19% national rise) trace back to Schwarz (1951), a movement publication, and to a diploma thesis. No audited series supports them, and key records were destroyed (September, 2026). Fisher (1933), a sympathetic contemporary, records only about 30–50 permanent jobs created. The works were also financed partly by outside subsidies and a 12,000-schilling provincial credit (von Muralt, 1934, via Greco, 2002). The money was accepted mainly because residents owed tax arrears payable in it. Defensible wording: *contemporary and later accounts report public-works employment and recovered tax revenue during the 14-month experiment, but the data are fragmentary, and whether demurrage itself (rather than tax-receivability and outside funds) drove the gains is disputed.*
2. **Wörgl, termination.** "National authorities shut it down in 1933" is broadly right but imprecise. The Austrian National Bank objected under Article 122 of its statute, the district and provincial supervisory authorities ordered a halt, and the scrip was withdrawn on 1 September 1933. The Administrative Court in Vienna rejected the town's appeal; WU Wien places this in mid-September 1933, and Champ (2008) says November 1933. Cite both and note the discrepancy.
3. **Gesell, "functioning as continuous gradual redistribution of capital without punitive taxation."** Gesell's own rationale, and Keynes's reading of it, centred on breaking the liquidity premium of money so that interest falls and capital accumulates. It did not centre on redistribution. Demurrage *is* a tax on holdings, so "without resorting to punitive taxation" is rhetorical. Keynes's substitution objection (holders shift to other liquid assets) should be cited.
4. **CNAS Sovereign AI Index, "21 such projects as of June 2026."** Not found. The current index (CNAS, Aug. 2026) tracks 184 projects in 67 countries. It says *most model projects with disclosed bases* fine-tune foreign open-weight models, and that from-scratch models are about 40% of model projects. Replace the number, or cite the exact earlier CNAS release if "21" came from one; I did not locate any such release.
5. **OECD (2025).** The citation is accurate. The bid-rigging point refers to infrastructure *construction tenders*, and the OECD rates collusion risk as low at the frontier and higher in commoditised layers. Say "flags a prospective risk", not that collusion is occurring. Full citation: OECD Roundtables on Competition Policy Papers No. 330, doi:10.1787/623d1874-en.
6. **"Hoang et al., arXiv:"** The authors are **Sim, Zhang, Chan & Low (2020)**, ICML, PMLR 119, 8927–8936. The paper values *coalitions* by information gain on model parameters, then allocates rewards by the **Shapley value**, paid in noisy *models*, not money. Lindley (1956) and MacKay (1992) are the right foundational citations for information gain as data value. Note the competing Shapley approach (Ghorbani & Zou, 2019; Jia et al., 2019).
7. **Shannon (1948) as the basis for a unit of information *processed*.** Shannon defines a unit of information (the bit), not of computation, and explicitly brackets meaning. Landauer (1961) and Lloyd (2000) show that information processing has physical energy floors. "Substrate-agnostic" holds for the *definition* of the unit, not for its *cost*. MLPerf shows that reference workloads measure time-to-quality on a fixed task, not "information processed."
8. **Hardware claims.** Replace VGTimes with TrendForce (Sept. 30, 2026) and Micron (Dec. 3, 2025). Replace Medium/Hassanali with TrendForce News (Dec. 26, 2025), and describe the ~20% as an *industry estimate of wafer-equivalent share of DRAM supply*, relayed from *Commercial Times*. "Pulling supply away from the consumer market entirely" is not supported; Micron's exit from the Crucial brand is the documented fact. Replace "Tech Insider": the Nvidia claim is single-source trade reporting (via *The Information*), and "completed design shelved" is unverified. Soften it or drop it.
9. **Quantity theory.** The chapter invokes it without a citation. Cite Fisher (1911/1920) for MV = PT. Note that the inflation argument applies to a *fixed nominal* compute unit; under share-indexing, the per-person allocation is a share, not a stock, and the real issue becomes what a share buys.
10. **Rural electrification analogy.** Kitchens & Fishback (2015) and Lewis & Severnini (2020) support it, but the REA worked through low-interest *loans* to member-owned cooperatives, which were largely repaid, not through relief payments. Its non-farm effects were small. The chapter's "alongside relief payments" framing should cite the REA as a credit-and-cooperative model.
11. **"Company scrip" framing (Ch. 3 opening).** Fishback (1986, 1992) finds that miners were generally not in debt to company stores, that store prices were close to independent stores, and that scrip functioned as a wage advance. The harm depended on the issuer's local monopsony, not on scrip as such. The chapter's point holds only if framed as *issuer control plus lack of exit*.

## Synthesis notes

**Compute as the unit of account.** The strongest case for compute as a unit rests on Sastry et al. (2024): compute is unusually quantifiable, detectable, and excludable. It also rests on MLPerf (Mattson et al., 2020; Reddi et al., 2020), which shows that rule-bound reference workloads can compare radically different hardware. Shannon gives the conceptual precedent for a medium-independent unit. The case against is that what can be measured is hardware throughput or time-to-quality on a fixed task, not "information processed." Training-compute accounting is not standardised (Sastry et al.). FLOPs are not even uniform across precisions (Hobbhahn et al., 2023). Landauer and Lloyd tie every operation to physical energy, so the unit's cost is substrate-dependent even if its definition is not. Productivity also grows fast: price-performance doubles roughly every 2.1 years. A compute unit therefore has to be re-based as the reference workload ages, which is a governance problem as hard as central banking. Hayek (1976) would add that a single mandated unit forgoes the discipline of currency competition.

**Indexing to a per-person share of global compute.** Fisher's equation of exchange explains why a fixed-quantity unit would lose value as capacity grows, and indexing to a share solves the nominal-erosion problem neatly. The best evidence that large unconditional transfers need not be inflationary or destroy work comes from Egger et al. (2022): a 2.5 multiplier and about 0.1% price effects in a slack economy. Jones & Marinescu (2022) find no aggregate employment effect from Alaska's dividend, and Verho et al. (2022) find no employment change in Finland. Against this, Vivalt et al. (2024/2026) find a real, moderate fall in labour supply in the US. Hoynes & Rothstein (2019) stress cost and targeting. Egger's result depends on spare capacity, while compute is currently supply-constrained (TrendForce 2026). Korinek & Stiglitz (2019) and Korinek & Suh (2024) give the strongest rationale: if automation outruns capital accumulation, wages can collapse, and a dividend on the dominant factor becomes the main income channel. Piketty's r > g makes the same point for capital generally.

**Split-at-source survival tier.** Currie & Gahvari (2008) supply the theory for in-kind or earmarked transfers: Samaritan's dilemma, self-targeting, and political economy. Haushofer & Shapiro (2016) find that monthly flows protect food security better than lump sums. Thaler's mental accounting suggests labels change use. Section 254's "evolving level" of universal service is a legal precedent for a proportional, technology-relative floor. Against it, Van Parijs & Vanderborght define basic income as unconditional and in cash, so earmarking is a departure that must be defended as more than paternalism. Fishback's company-store work warns that non-transferable units are harmful when one issuer controls the goods they buy. The survival tier needs multiple providers and portability (Narechania & Sitaraman, 2024; Mueller, 1997, on how "universal service" once entrenched a monopoly).

**Demurrage on the discretionary tier.** Keynes judged the idea sound. Buiter & Panigirtzoglou (2003) and Goodfriend (2000) formalise carry taxes as legitimate monetary tools, and digital ledgers remove the stamp-handling costs that sank US scrip (Champ, 2008). The empirical record is thin. Wörgl's data are fragmentary and confounded by tax-receivability and subsidies (Greco, 2002; September, 2026). Godschalk (2012) finds the demurrage rate is not crucial in local currencies, and Gelleri's Chiemgauer evidence is self-reported. Keynes's substitution argument is the decisive theoretical objection: a holding cost on compute balances pushes accumulation into hardware, equity, and land. The draft admits this, so demurrage blunts idle hoarding but cannot by itself prevent concentration. Pairing it with asset-side rules (the "Free Land" half of Gesell's programme, or structural separation) is the logical extension.

**Pricing data by information gain.** Lindley (1956), MacKay (1992), Settles (2009), and Sim et al. (2020) ground information gain as the value of data. It rewards undersampled lives rather than celebrity, which is the dissertation's normative aim. Against it, information gain is relative to the model's prior and hypothesis space, so a misspecified model misprices novelty (MacKay). Value is combinatorial and data is replicable (Agarwal et al., 2019). Shapley-based approaches (Ghorbani & Zou; Jia et al.) define value differently, and influence-function work (Koh & Liang, 2017) shows that payments can be gamed through poisoning or manufactured novelty.

**Physical delivery and concentration.** The OECD (2025), Narechania & Sitaraman (2024), Vipra & Korinek (2023), Lehdonvirta et al. (2024), and CNAS (2026) all document concentrated layers and uneven geography, and Micron and TrendForce document consumer-supply displacement. Kitchens & Fishback (2015) and Lewis & Severnini (2020) show that a cooperative-credit build-out closed an access gap at low net fiscal cost with long-run benefits. That is the best historical support for Problem 4. The OECD's warning that large public infrastructure tenders invite bid-rigging should be written into the proposal.
